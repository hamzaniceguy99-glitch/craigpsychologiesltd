How we work

Evidence, consent, and no promised outcomes

A report full of percentile scores and no explanation helps nobody. The useful part is the account of how this particular child learns, what gets in the way, and what the adults around them can do differently — written so a teacher and a parent can both act on it.

That means recommendations have to be achievable in a real classroom with thirty children in it. Advice that assumes one-to-one support that doesn't exist reads well and changes nothing.

It also means being honest about what an assessment can't deliver. Families under pressure are sometimes offered confident predictions about plans, placements and funding. Those decisions sit with local authorities and tribunals, and promising them would be both wrong and unkind.
